# Resolution-level research pilot

Date: 2 November 2026  
Scope: six existing unresolved records, selected to test different portal and evidence problems without discarding the earlier discovery work.

## Allocation

Three `gpt-6.1-sol` workers at medium reasoning handled two authorities each. Each worker was limited to eight targeted searches and two browser attempts per authority, was required to start from the existing CSV row, and could only treat an explicit resolution, current constitutional text or official operational confirmation as decisive.

| Worker task | Authorities | Reason for selection |
|---|---|---|
| A | Isles of Scilly; Chichester | Third-party approval report versus an indexed ModernGov agenda |
| B | Isle of Wight; Gosport | Operational evidence versus an Azure-WAF-blocked Council portal |
| C | Dorset; Southampton | Monitoring Officer route versus an Imperva-blocked and initially misidentified portal |

## Results retained in the dataset

| Authority | Before | After | Decisive result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isles of Scilly | Outcome unconfirmed | Adoption evidenced | Official post-meeting page confirms Full Council approval on 15 October |
| Isle of Wight | Outcome unconfirmed | Implemented; resolution not located | Current official page describes the live schedules, gateway and nominated roles |
| Gosport | Outcome unconfirmed | Outcome unconfirmed | Agenda and report found; decision, calendar and document routes blocked |
| Dorset | Outcome unconfirmed | Outcome unconfirmed | Committee report established consultation rather than adoption; later text not retrievable |
| Chichester | Outcome unconfirmed | Outcome unconfirmed | Cabinet only recommended onward approval; Council resolution remained inaccessible |
| Southampton | Outcome unconfirmed | Outcome unconfirmed | Correct official portal and report URLs found, but Imperva blocked substantive retrieval |

All six CSV records were enriched with the strongest source URLs, a more precise evidence type, and access-limitation wording even where the status did not change.

## Visible token count

Counts use the `o200k_base` tokenizer over the exact delegated prompt and final returned text. They do **not** include hidden reasoning, search-result payloads, browser content, tool-call arguments or provider-side overhead, which are not exposed by the delegation interface. They are therefore reproducible visible-message counts, not billing totals.

| Worker task | Prompt tokens | Return tokens | Visible total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A — Scilly + Chichester | 340 | 826 | 1,166 |
| B — Isle of Wight + Gosport | 339 | 710 | 1,049 |
| C — Dorset + Southampton | 335 | 811 | 1,146 |
| **Total** | **1,014** | **2,347** | **3,361** |

Average visible delegation traffic was about 560 tokens per authority.

## Reported research operations

| Worker task | Search queries | Page/fetch operations | Terminal fetches | Browser attempts |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 10 | 16 pages opened | 1 | 1 |
| B | 12 | 16 pages opened | 1 | 2 |
| C | 16 | 32 web fetches, including repeats | 6 | 4 |

The browser attempts confirmed that WAF access is a genuine limiting condition; a browser is not by itself a universal solution. The useful improvement came from making the evidence chain and stop conditions explicit. This allocation is suitable as an escalation tier after cheap discovery, rather than as the first pass over every authority.
